# 1. One Frozen Bet

**Commit to a single direction. Tune the variables around it, never the core, until a real test reads at sample size.**

## The problem this solves

The most common failure mode in early work isn't a bad idea. It's running four ideas for one week each and concluding, every time, that "it didn't work." Nothing reached a sample size large enough to actually tell you anything. You were measuring noise and reacting to it.

Every pivot resets the clock to zero. If you pivot faster than your test can produce a signal, you will never see a signal, no matter how many pivots you make.

## The rule

Pick one bet. Freeze its **core** — the thing you're actually testing. Then separate the core from the **variables**:

- **The core** is the hypothesis. The offer, the audience, the mechanism. You do **not** touch this mid-test.
- **The variables** are everything around it: the wording, the channel, the targeting, the price point, the order of steps. These you tune freely.

When results come back weak, the instinct is to change the core. That instinct is almost always wrong, because you can't yet tell whether the core is bad or whether a variable was off. Tune the variables first. Only kill the core when a test ran to sample size and still failed.

## What "frozen" means in practice

- No changing the bet because a single data point looked bad.
- No changing the bet because something newer looks shinier.
- No changing the bet because waiting is uncomfortable.
- Changing the bet is allowed exactly once: **after** a full test reaches sample size and reads as a real failure.

## The honest version

Freezing a bet feels like missing out on the better idea you just thought of. It isn't. The "better idea" is untested too, and switching to it just restarts the clock you were already failing to beat. Discipline here is not stubbornness. It's the only way to ever get a clean read on anything.

## Checklist

- [ ] I can state my bet in one sentence.
- [ ] I've written down what is core (frozen) and what is a variable (tunable).
- [ ] I've defined the sample size that counts as a real test **before** running it.
- [ ] When I'm tempted to pivot, I check: did a full test actually read, or am I just impatient?

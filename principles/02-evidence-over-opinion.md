# 2. Evidence Over Opinion

**Decisions come from data with sources. "No data found" triggers research, never a guess.**

## The problem this solves

Opinion is fast, free, and usually wrong at the exact moments it matters most. When a decision is hard, it's tempting to fill the gap with a confident-sounding take. That take feels like analysis. It isn't. It's a guess wearing a suit.

## The rule

For any decision that actually matters — what to build, who to sell to, which channel, which price — the answer has to come from one of:

- Real data you collected or can point to.
- Primary sources with numbers, named people, or named companies.
- Published research, case studies, or tool data you can cite.

If none of those exist yet, the correct answer is **"no data found"** — followed by going to get some. It is never "here's my best guess" dressed up as a conclusion.

## Opinion vs. evidence

| Looks like analysis | Actually is |
|---|---|
| "Users probably want X." | Opinion. No source. |
| "This channel is better." | Opinion, unless you have the numbers. |
| "In a survey of N people, X% said..." (with the source) | Evidence. |
| "Three operators doing this report..." (named, cited) | Evidence. |

## Label everything

When you do have to move without full data — and sometimes you do — say so out loud. Mark assumptions as assumptions. State your confidence level. Name the gap. An unlabeled assumption is the thing that quietly becomes "a fact everyone knows" three weeks later, and nobody remembers it was a guess.

## The honest version

You will not always have data, and you can't let that paralyze you. The rule isn't "never move without perfect evidence." It's "never *pretend* a guess is evidence." Move on a labeled assumption when you must. Just don't lie to yourself about what it is.

## Checklist

- [ ] For this decision, can I point to a source?
- [ ] If not, have I said "no data found" instead of inventing one?
- [ ] Are my assumptions labeled as assumptions, with a stated confidence level?
- [ ] Did I note the gaps someone else would need to know about?
